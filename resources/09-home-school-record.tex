\toolsection{The Existing Camp Artifact}

The final camp communication package was a personalized full-week recap book for each of the team's assigned youth. One completed PDF contains five landscape pages for Days 1--5. Each attended day receives its own page and reconstructs the learner's experience through an action image, an exact or transcript-grounded contribution, a concise science story, and a five-activity memory trail showing what the learner did and how the evidence was interpreted \citep{campEvidence2026}. Science moves such as observing, modeling, testing, asking, and acting help families see how participation developed across the week. Each activity is connected to why it mattered, while an explicit evidence limit separates what the records establish from what remains uncertain. The result gives the learner and family a concrete visual record they can point to, retell, question, or correct together. Personalized drafts invite families to review the work and experience with the learner. The production archive confirms completed souvenirs, daily and weekly recaps, review renders, source records, image manifests, and drafts. The personalized packages were completed and prepared for family and collaborators' delivery.

\toolsection{From Rating Log to Family Learning Story}

The Daily Communication Logs packet offers useful structures for home--school communication, but several examples foreground good/fair/poor days, warnings, sitting, compliance, or adult initials. The completed camp recap books demonstrate a different communication architecture: they organize the record around what the learner did, communicated, questioned, contributed, and was still working to understand.

\toolsection{Prepare, Review, and Deliver}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Verify identity and access.} Confirm attendance, name, pronouns, language, intended readers, and permissions.
  \item \textbf{Assemble the evidence.} Gather student memos, artifacts, recap materials, images, and activity sequence.
  \item \textbf{Separate evidence from interpretation.} Mark exact words, documented actions, team evidence, interpretations, and unknowns before designing the page.
  \item \textbf{Return control to the learner.} When possible, let the learner select, edit, translate, restrict, replace, or withhold parts of the outgoing story.
  \item \textbf{Review the complete record.} Check every attended day, all activity cards, attribution, accessibility, privacy, and evidence limits before export.
  \item \textbf{Deliver without overclaiming.} Use an approved communication channel, document receipt when available, and invite a low-burden response. Without evidence of delivery, say \textit{prepared for family delivery}, not \textit{sent}.
\end{enumerate}

\toolsection{Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS in Practice}

The Puzzle Plan carries the student memo, daily souvenir, team recap, activity evidence, and week-long storyline forward without collapsing them into one adult judgment. Each source contributes a different piece of the learner's experience. EQUITAS then asks who controls the family narrative, whose activities or communication routes are missing, whether interpretation is clearly labeled, whether uncertainty remains visible, and whether the family has an accessible way to return knowledge to the team. Family knowledge is part of the evidence system \citep{zacarian2015community}.

\reflectionbox{\textbf{Artifact and delivery proof:} The private production bundle contains two completed personalized recap PDFs---a page for each learner whose documented participation began on Day 1 or 2---along with review renders, source records, image manifests, and personalized drafts.\citep{campEvidence2026}.}

\adaptationbox{Collect only information that supports learning, communication, access, or continuity; follow privacy policy; use approved channels; and honor preferences for translation, audio, paper, event-triggered updates, weekly summaries, no photographs, no specific message, or no formal log at all.}
